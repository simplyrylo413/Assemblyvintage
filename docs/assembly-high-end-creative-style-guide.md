# Assembly High-End Creative Style Guide

Use this system for Assembly event carousels, paid-social ads, posters, vendor announcements, and other image-led campaign creative unless Albert asks for a different direction.

## Locked hero typography — Editorial Italic, choice 3

Albert explicitly selected **choice 3, Editorial Italic** on October 26, 2026 as part of Assembly's design language for static images. This selection supersedes experimental sculpted 3D, whimsical swash, and offset-upright type mockups.

- Use oversized, tall, high-contrast fashion-editorial **italic serif** hero lettering, close to Bodoni Moda Italic.
- Give the headline a gentle rising/off-axis angle of about 2–4 degrees and a **slight, soft charcoal drop shadow** for separation from the photo.
- Keep clean letterforms and tight, intentional stacking. Convey fun through witty short copy, italic movement and subtle composition; retain elegance for Assembly's core audience of Florida women aged 35–50.
- Do not add ornate swashes/curls, cartoon bounce, chunky 3D extrusion, red side faces, inflated letters, or deep theatrical shadows. Those explorations were rejected as insufficiently elegant.
- Keep every italic letter and period within safe margins; never clip the left edge of an italic R or its descender/terminal.
- Use the actual WHITE Assembly arch-and-wordmark brandmark with Vintage Market beneath, on a transparent background directly over the photo. This rule remains mandatory.
- Use a different supplied source photo for each of a four-image set unless Albert asks otherwise. Preserve real people, outfits and merchandise; do not fabricate poses or stock.
- Run NEW hero copy by Albert before making artwork. Once approved, retain the exact wording and proceed without asking again.
- Current approved four-line campaign set: `GO OFF RACK.`; `JUST LOOKING? SURE.`; `TRY. TWIRL. REPEAT.`; `SUNDAY, WELL SPENT.`.
- Keep event information in clean upright sans-serif type, so the headline is distinct from the supporting copy. Keep the compact oxblood event band and current Eventbrite CTA.

## Default static-image style — approved October 26, 2026

Use the approved `GO OFF RACK.` clothing-focused poster with the white, transparent-background Assembly brandmark as the default for ALL future Assembly static images, including standalone event posts and carousel covers. This latest direction supersedes the older quiet, boxed carousel treatment where they conflict.

- Make the clothes the star: full-bleed real Assembly photography, close decisive crops, rich garment color, visible fabric and styling details. Remove floor, bags and dead space when they distract. Do not invent inventory or replace supplied clothes.
- Build a dynamic, scroll-stopping first frame: oversized Editorial Italic headline, asymmetric but disciplined placement, strong contrast and minimal empty panels. Retain the premium editorial character rather than generic social templates.
- ALWAYS use the actual Assembly brandmark: upright arch above the official Assembly wordmark, with Vintage Market beneath. Never substitute a typeset Assembly name alone. Retrieve the official logo asset from `public/assets/assembly-logo-final.png` and preserve its exact geometry and proportions; composite the actual asset when needed for fidelity.
- Render the brandmark WHITE with a TRANSPARENT background directly over the photograph. No ivory rectangle, plaque, badge or background box. Maintain breathing room and readable contrast.
- Keep the ink / ivory / oxblood visual system, oversized high-contrast serif display type and clean sans-serif event information. Use a compact oxblood event band so photography dominates.
- Include date, time, venue and admission on the opening event card. Standalone posters must contain all required event details and the current ticket CTA. For this November campaign use `FREE TICKETS: EVENTBRITE LINK IN BIO`, not the earlier website CTA.
- Standalone images have no carousel page number; use numbering only when actually building a carousel.
- Vary taglines and close crops while retaining this same brandmark, typography hierarchy, photograph-first energy and event band.
- Do not freeze November's date into future campaigns: verify current event information for every new event.
- QA the actual final export for official logo fidelity, white mark with no background box, clothes prominence, readable event details and correct CTA.

## Positioning

Assembly should feel like a curated fashion and culture event—not a craft fair, flea-market flyer, or cheerful neighborhood pop-up.

The visual benchmark combines:

- The institutional confidence, oversized typography, disciplined grid, and documentary event photography used by Not-A-Normal / NORMAL.
- The cinematic crops, archive language, fashion-editorial typography, and restrained palette used by New York Vintage.
- Assembly's own warmth, real community, jewelry, independent dealers, and South Florida setting.

Reference accounts:

- https://www.instagram.com/normal.nyc/
- https://www.normalnewyork.com/
- https://www.instagram.com/newyorkvintageinc/

## Visual principles

1. **Image first.** Use one strong fashion, jewelry, or event image as the dominant subject. Crop with conviction.
2. **Restrained hierarchy.** One editorial headline, one concise supporting line, and one compact information block per card.
3. **Fashion publication, not template.** Use negative space, strict alignment, thin rules, page numbering, and quiet metadata.
4. **Real texture.** Preserve skin, fabric, jewelry, film grain, venue atmosphere, and imperfect candid moments.
5. **Confident simplicity.** Remove anything that does not increase desire, credibility, or event clarity.

## Photography

Prioritize real Assembly photography whenever available.

Preferred subjects:

- Tight jewelry and accessory details.
- Stylish shoppers interacting naturally.
- Candid vendor and customer moments.
- Wide venue images that establish scale and curation.
- Singular fashion looks with clean silhouettes.

Treatment:

- Warm neutral or restrained black-and-white grade.
- Moderate desaturation; protect skin tone and garment detail.
- Dark, cinematic overlays only when needed for legibility.
- Light organic grain is acceptable.
- Use tightly considered crops rather than multiple small decorative cutouts.

Generated people are allowed only when real photography cannot support the concept. Generated work must look like natural editorial photography: realistic skin texture, anatomy, hands, fabric, jewelry construction, reflections, and asymmetrical human posing. Reject anything glossy, over-retouched, uncanny, or visibly synthetic.

## Typography

### Editorial display

Use Bodoni Moda for fashion-led headlines, dates, and single-word statements.

- Source: https://github.com/indestructible-type/Bodoni
- Prefer regular or italic at large sizes.
- Use short lines and preserve generous margins.
- Do not use decorative serif type for paragraphs or dense event details.

### Information and utility

Use Bricolage Grotesque for mastheads, labels, dates, venue details, and calls to action.

- Source: https://github.com/ateliertriay/bricolage
- Use regular for supporting copy.
- Use bold or condensed bold for compact all-caps information.
- Increase tracking for small labels and metadata.

## Layout rules

- Default canvas: 1080 × 1350.
- Keep critical copy at least 60 px from outer edges.
- Use the actual white Assembly arch-and-wordmark brandmark on a transparent background over the photograph, with `VINTAGE MARKET` beneath and a thin horizontal rule. Do not replace the brandmark with a typeset name.
- Optional page numbers: `01 / 04`, `02 / 04`, etc.
- Use thin 1–2 px rules rather than stickers, outlines, or decorative blobs.
- Use full-bleed photographs or one deliberate editorial split.
- Calls to action may use a single oxblood bar on ivory or white typography on a dark image.
- Date, time, venue, and admission must remain legible when the card is viewed at approximately 25% scale.

## Copy system

Voice: selective, concise, fashion-aware, culturally confident, and welcoming without sounding mass-market.

Approved patterns:

- `FOUND ONCE. WORN FOREVER.`
- `THE EDIT`
- `ASSEMBLE.`
- `A curated Sunday of vintage fashion, rare jewelry and independent dealers.`
- `RESERVE FREE ADMISSION`

Prefer:

- `curated`, `selected`, `singular`, `rare`, `independent dealers`, `the edit`, `in person`, `archive`, `one-of-one`.

Avoid:

- Hype-heavy social copy, excessive emojis, exclamation points, fake scarcity, or unsupported luxury claims.
- Generic lines such as `shop till you drop`, `something for everyone`, or `don't miss out`.
- Long paragraphs embedded in the artwork.

## Carousel narrative

Use this four-card structure as the default paid-social template:

1. **Desire:** emotionally resonant hook over a strong fashion or jewelry image.
2. **Curation:** what the audience will discover, expressed as an edit rather than an inventory list.
3. **Information:** date, time, venue, and free admission/parking on one highly legible card.
4. **Action:** community image or quiet poster treatment with one RSVP instruction.

## Do not use

- Bright yellow stickers or multiple competing accent colors.
- Hand-drawn arrows, doodles, tape effects, or scrapbook collage as the default campaign language.
- Rounded social-media template cards.
- Dense lists, oversized hashtags, or emojis inside the artwork.
- More than two type families.
- AI-looking models, plastic skin, impossible jewelry, malformed hands, or synthetic fabric texture.
- Stock photography when relevant Assembly photography exists.
- A direct copy of another market's logo, exact layout, or proprietary campaign artwork.

## Final QA

Before delivery, verify:

- Correct current event date, time, venue, admission, parking, and CTA.
- 1080 × 1350 dimensions for every feed card.
- No clipped typography, collisions, or text crossing an important face/product detail.
- Readability at thumbnail size.
- Real Assembly images remain recognizable and undistorted.
- The sequence feels like one campaign, not four unrelated posts.
- The final pack includes upload-ready JPGs and lossless PNG masters.
